# On the planes of Cartan’s cubics and the missing seven

The real part of a complex cube has a radial gradient norm. If several such fields are coupled by real trilinear terms on distinct triples of fields, the gradient norm can still be radial only for one, four or thirteen fields. The four- and thirteen-field models are trace cubics on traceless Hermitian matrices over the complex numbers and the octonions, respectively. The quaternionic candidate is excluded by measuring how much of the product of two planes projects onto a third.

The results bring together eikonal cubics and isoparametric hypersurfaces, mutually unbiased bases in quantum information, and frame geometry in the compact Albert algebra. The quaternionic result lowers the bound in dimension three from seven to five and bears on Chterental and Đoković’s question about maximal families. In the Albert algebra, the triple shares distinguish an orbit of tight codes in the octonionic projective plane and relate the frame systems to orthogonal Cartan decompositions. [Chterental–Đoković, Theorems 5.1 and 5.4 and Problem 6.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.4051v1), [McNulty–Weigert, §9.3](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2026-04-01-2051), [Cohn–Kumar–Minton, §8.2 and Theorem 8.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3188v4), [Kostrikin–Tiep, pp. 1–3 and Theorem 3.1.2](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110901757).

The companion paper of the same title is attached to the [v1.0.0 release](https://github.com/macgregor-sudo/cartan-cubic-planes/releases/tag/v1.0.0) of this repository, as a PDF and its TeX source, and has been submitted to Hexagon. Its review of earlier work found no earlier statement of the four-or-thirteen restriction for coupled fields, the quaternionic triple-share lower bound, or the five-basis bound in quaternionic dimension three. Its introduction gives the scope of that search and cites the closest results.

## Cubics, planes and shares

A symmetric real trilinear form $`d`$ on a finite-dimensional Euclidean space gives a cubic $`W(x)=d(x,x,x)`$ and a product determined by $`\langle x\circ y,z\rangle=d(x,y,z)`$. The eikonal condition of strength $`c>0`$ is $`\|x\circ x\|^2=c\|x\|^4`$. Harmonicity means each multiplication map $`y\mapsto x\circ y`$ has trace zero. A closed subspace contains the products of its elements. A complete closed plane system is a pairwise orthogonal family of closed real two-planes whose sum is the ambient space.

For two orthogonal closed planes of a positive-strength eikonal cubic, the space $`M_{ij}`$ spanned by their mixed products is four-dimensional. For three distinct planes in such an orthogonal system the geometric share is $`s_{ijk}=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(P_kQ_{ij})`$, where $`P_k`$ projects onto $`U_k`$ and $`Q_{ij}`$ onto $`M_{ij}`$. The library proves symmetry in the three indices. The sum statements below use completeness. [Mixed products and plane shares](src/ThirteenPlanes/PlaneShares.lean).

On a complete system let $`W_{\mathrm{diag}}`$ be the sum of the single-plane restrictions, each evaluated on the corresponding orthogonal projection, and write $`W_t=W_{\mathrm{diag}}+t(W-W_{\mathrm{diag}})`$. The energy at $`x`$ is $`\|\nabla W_t(x)\|^2/(9c)`$. In Lean this is the squared norm of the pencil’s quadratic product map divided by $`c`$.

A Jordan frame consists of three trace-one idempotents whose pairwise Jordan products vanish and whose sum is the unit. Two frames are unbiased when all nine cross overlaps for the Jordan trace pairing are $`1/3`$. Their traceless planes are the spans of the idempotents with one third of the unit subtracted. In quaternionic dimension three, the columns of a unitary matrix give an orthonormal basis, and their rank-one projectors give the frame. Two such bases are unbiased when every squared quaternionic inner-product norm across their columns is $`1/3`$.

## Trace formulas and the attaining triple

For quaternionic bases $`U,V,W`$ with rank-one projectors $`P_a,Q_b,R_c`$, the compared trace share is $`6\sum_{a,b,c}\bigl(\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}((P_a\circ Q_b)\circ R_c)-1/9\bigr)^2`$. The library theorem `QuaternionicMUB.basisGeometricShare_eq_basisS` identifies it with the projection share for pairwise unbiased bases. Write $`\omega=-1/2+i\sqrt3/2`$ and $`F_{ab}=\omega^{ab}/\sqrt3`$ for $`a,b\in\{0,1,2\}`$. The balance of a unit quaternion $`q=x+yi+zj+wk`$ is $`x^2+y^2-z^2-w^2`$. For the attaining triple take $`r=\sqrt{\sqrt3}`$, $`A=(r+(r^2-1)/\sqrt2)/2`$ and $`C=(r-(r^2-1)/\sqrt2)/2`$. Its phase vector is $`(A+Cj,\ \omega^2(1-j)/\sqrt2,\ -\omega(C+Aj))`$. [Compared definitions](src/CartanCubicPlanes/Challenge.lean), [Quaternionic share identity](src/ThirteenPlanes/Quaternionic/NativeFrameShare.lean).

For the Albert comparison the same formula uses the real Jordan trace on the three frame idempotents. The proof library identifies it with the geometric share through `Challenge.albertFrameShare_eq_nativeProjection`. For any thirteen pairwise unbiased Albert frames, the corresponding planes form a complete orthogonal system, so the general compared support bound applies. The Albert comparison gives attainment and the single orbit at 52. The proof library establishes both trace-to-projection identities outside the thirteen comparisons. [Albert share identity](src/AlbertSparseAttainmentSolution.lean), [Support bound](src/ThirteenPlanes/FrameSupportCounting.lean).

## Results in the proof library

The marked harmonic classification identifies the strength-one cubics of dimension at least three with the real, complex, quaternionic and octonionic Hermitian models. The complex complete plane system is unique up to one unitary conjugation and relabelling. The field defect theorem gives an attained positive deviation from constant unit-sphere energy for each excluded field count, a seven-field example with defect $`3/4`$, and a positive lower bound on the maximum cross-overlap discrepancy for six quaternionic frames. In the quaternionic capacity proof six unbiased frames would extend to seven. For a fixed pair, the other five shares would each exceed $`2/5`$, contradicting the pair sum. [Complex MUB classification, Brierley–Weigert–Bengtsson, §2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4097v2), [Field defect](src/ThirteenPlanes/FieldDefect/Main.lean), [Quaternionic capacity](src/ThirteenPlanes/Quaternionic/CapacityCorollaries.lean).

At 52 positive shares every share is zero or one. Every pair then lies in exactly one closed quartet, and the quartets are the lines of a projective plane isomorphic to $`\mathrm{PG}(2,3)`$. The proof library also establishes the converses from these incidence conditions. The printed proof uses the projective-plane classification from Hall’s *Combinatorial Theory*, second edition, and Hughes and Piper’s *Projective Planes*. The compact first-Tits model supplies Fourier idempotents, the Weyl multiplication table on each line and a cube-root phase formula for the cubic. Two orthogonal closed planes generate a complex Hermitian Jordan subalgebra through their frames. Adding a third orthogonal closed plane gives a complex Hermitian, quaternionic Hermitian or full Albert subalgebra. For each pair in a complete system, at least seven of the eleven choices of third plane generate the full Albert algebra. Rotating the four frames on a graded line by an automorphism of its Hermitian subalgebra preserves completeness, and preserves the minimum precisely when it permutes the four line planes. The compact complex and quaternionic matrix Cartans fix traceless diagonal planes up to unitary conjugacy. A Casimir identity turns a Killing-orthogonal Cartan decomposition into an orthogonal sum of these fixed planes. In particular a compact $`\mathfrak{sp}(3)`$ decomposition would yield seven unbiased quaternionic frames. For the complex symplectic algebra the obstruction applies to decompositions permuted by a compact conjugation. For compact $`\mathfrak f_4`$, every Cartan is of frame type. It fixes a two-dimensional frame plane on the traceless Albert representation, with operator-trace Casimir one sixth of the projector onto its orthogonal complement. The thirteen fixed planes of an orthogonal Cartan decomposition are closed and fill the representation orthogonally. Their frames are pairwise unbiased. The ternary Albert grading gives one such decomposition, and line rotations yield inequivalent decompositions when they change the line planes. In the cyclic octonion coordinates $`(2,3),(3,1),(1,2)`$ the diagonal-frame stabilizer obeys $`(\alpha_1 a)(\alpha_2 b)=\overline{\alpha_3(\overline{ab})}`$. ## Proof sketch

Polarizing the eikonal square law at a unit idempotent gives the marked Peirce decomposition. A closed plane exposes three off-diagonal spaces with a bilinear product composing norms. Hurwitz’s theorem restricts their dimensions and the marked cubic identifies the Hermitian models. For a pair in a complete orthogonal system of closed planes of positive eikonal strength, the mixed-product space lies in the orthogonal complement of the pair and has dimension four. The remaining plane projectors resolve the identity on that complement, so their half-traces against the mixed-product projector add to two. The quaternionic trace formula reduces the lower bound to an inequality for the three Fourier phase balances. For thirteen planes, the total share is 52. The upper bound of one forces at least 52 positive terms and forces each positive term to be one at equality.

In the Albert orbit proof the four-dimensional product space identifies the other two planes in the closed quartet through a pair. In the compact first-Tits model, an automorphism normalizes a pair of a reference quartet to the diagonal and Fourier frames. An automorphism fixing the quartet algebra puts an exterior frame in standard position. The eigenframes of the order-three elements on the lines through that frame determine the remaining planes. For completion, the two-dimensional orthogonal complement of the twelve traceless planes is closed. The proof diagonalizes one element and places both generators in the same frame. [Orbit recognition](src/ThirteenPlanes/AlbertGrading/Recognition/CoreMain.lean), [Completion](src/ThirteenPlanes/AlbertGrading/Completion/PairFrame.lean).

For the energy pencil, extrema recover the planes in the unlabelled case, polarized mixed responses recover the parameter, and product compatibility forces a common sign. The native endpoint witness reverses the cubic on one plane by an orthogonal pullback. In the Cartan construction the operator trace pairs opposite derivation grades and compact conjugation exchanges the grades in each pair. Their real descents give the thirteen Cartans. Tensor contractions restrict the one-loop equation to the two pencil coefficients, leaving a ratio equation and a strength equation.

## Mathematical sources

Cartan classified the harmonic eikonal cubics. Tkachev established the axial form for the nonharmonic case. Hurwitz’s composition theorem limits the possible dimensions of the marked slots, and the Lean argument uses the unital composition-algebra form discussed by Baez.
