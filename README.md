# On the planes of Cartan’s cubics and the missing seven

The real part of a complex cube, $`W=\mathrm{Re}\,z^3`$, has a gradient whose norm depends only on $`|z|`$. If several such fields are coupled by real trilinear terms on distinct triples of fields, the gradient norm can still depend only on the norm of the whole field configuration (the eikonal condition) only for one, four or thirteen fields. The four- and thirteen-field models are Cartan’s cubics, the trace cubics on traceless Hermitian $`3\times3`$ matrices over the complex numbers and the octonions, respectively. The quaternionic candidate, with seven fields, is excluded by measuring how much of the product of two planes projects onto a third.

The results bring together eikonal cubics and isoparametric hypersurfaces, mutually unbiased bases in quantum information, and frame geometry in the compact Albert algebra of Hermitian $`3\times3`$ octonionic matrices. The quaternionic result lowers the bound in dimension three from seven to five and bears on Chterental and Đoković’s question about maximal sets of mutually unbiased bases. In the Albert algebra, the shares distinguish an orbit of tight codes in the octonionic projective plane and relate the frame systems to orthogonal decompositions of compact $`\mathfrak f_4`$ into Cartan subalgebras. [Chterental–Đoković, Theorems 5.1 and 5.4 and Problem 6.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.4051v1), [McNulty–Weigert, §9.3](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2026-04-01-2051), [Cohn–Kumar–Minton, §8.2 and Theorem 8.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3188v4), [Kostrikin–Tiep, pp. 1–3 and Theorem 3.1.2](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110901757).

The paper of the same title is published on Hexagon as [2611.01171](https://hexagonmath.org/abs/2611.01171), and the releases of this repository attach it as a PDF with its TeX source. Its search of earlier work found no earlier statement of the four-or-thirteen restriction for coupled fields, the quaternionic lower bound on the share, or the five-basis bound in quaternionic dimension three. Its introduction gives the scope of that search and cites the closest results.

## Cubics, planes and shares

A symmetric real trilinear form $`d`$ on a finite-dimensional Euclidean space gives a cubic $`W(x)=d(x,x,x)`$ and a product, the cubic product, determined by $`\langle x\circ y,z\rangle=d(x,y,z)`$, so that $`\nabla W=3\,x\circ x`$. The cubic is eikonal with eikonal constant $`c>0`$ if $`\|x\circ x\|^2=c\|x\|^4`$. Harmonicity means each multiplication map $`y\mapsto x\circ y`$ has trace zero. A closed subspace contains the products of its elements. A plane is a closed two-dimensional subspace, and a complete system of planes is a pairwise orthogonal family of planes whose sum is the ambient space.

For two orthogonal planes $`U_i,U_j`$ of an eikonal cubic, the product space $`U_i\circ U_j`$ spanned by their products is four-dimensional. For three distinct planes in such an orthogonal system the share is $`s_{ijk}=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(P_kQ_{ij})`$, where $`\mathrm{Tr}`$ is the operator trace, $`P_k`$ projects onto $`U_k`$ and $`Q_{ij}`$ onto $`U_i\circ U_j`$. The library proves symmetry in the three indices. The sum statements below use completeness. [Products and shares](src/ThirteenPlanes/PlaneShares.lean).

On a complete system let $`W_{\mathrm{diag}}`$ be the sum of the single-plane restrictions, each evaluated on the corresponding orthogonal projection, and write $`W_t=W_{\mathrm{diag}}+t(W-W_{\mathrm{diag}})`$, the member of the pencil with ratio $`t`$ (the paper’s $`d_t`$). The energy of $`W_t`$ at $`x`$ is $`\|\nabla W_t(x)\|^2`$, and the normalized energy is $`\|\nabla W_t(x)\|^2/(9c)`$ (the paper’s $`f_t`$). In Lean the normalized energy is the squared norm of the pencil’s quadratic product map divided by $`c`$.

A Jordan frame consists of three trace-one idempotents whose pairwise Jordan products vanish and whose sum is the unit; in quantum terms it is a rank-one projective measurement with three outcomes. Here the Jordan product is $`X\bullet Y=\tfrac12(XY+YX)`$ and the trace form is $`\langle X,Y\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(X\bullet Y)`$, with $`\mathrm{tr}`$ the real part of the matrix trace, so that the unit has trace three. Two frames are unbiased when all nine cross overlaps for the trace form are $`1/3`$. The plane of a frame is the span of its idempotents with one third of the unit subtracted. In quaternionic dimension three, the columns of a quaternionic unitary matrix (an element of the compact symplectic group $`\mathrm{Sp}(3)`$) give an orthonormal basis, and their rank-one projectors give the frame. Two such bases are unbiased (mutually unbiased bases) when every squared quaternionic inner-product norm across their columns is $`1/3`$.

## Trace formulas and the attaining triple

For quaternionic bases $`U,V,W`$ with rank-one projectors $`P_a,Q_b,R_c`$, the compared trace formula for the share is $`6\sum_{a,b,c}\bigl(\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}((P_a\bullet Q_b)\bullet R_c)-1/9\bigr)^2`$. The library theorem `QuaternionicMUB.basisGeometricShare_eq_basisS` identifies it with the share for pairwise unbiased bases. Write $`\omega=-1/2+i\sqrt3/2`$ and $`F_{ab}=\omega^{ab}/\sqrt3`$ for $`a,b\in\{0,1,2\}`$. The balance of a unit quaternion $`q=x+yi+zj+wk`$ is $`x^2+y^2-z^2-w^2`$. For the attaining triple take $`r=\sqrt{\sqrt3}`$, $`A=(r+(r^2-1)/\sqrt2)/2`$ and $`C=(r-(r^2-1)/\sqrt2)/2`$. Its phase vector is $`(A+Cj,\ \omega^2(1-j)/\sqrt2,\ -\omega(C+Aj))`$. [Compared definitions](src/CartanCubicPlanes/Challenge.lean), [Quaternionic share identity](src/ThirteenPlanes/Quaternionic/NativeFrameShare.lean).

For the Albert comparison the same formula uses the real Jordan trace on the three frame idempotents. The proof library identifies it with the share through `Challenge.albertFrameShare_eq_nativeProjection`. For any thirteen pairwise unbiased Albert frames, the corresponding planes form a complete system, so the general compared support bound applies. The Albert comparison gives attainment and the single orbit at 52. The proof library establishes both trace-to-projection identities outside the thirteen comparisons. [Albert share identity](src/AlbertSparseAttainmentSolution.lean), [Support bound](src/ThirteenPlanes/FrameSupportCounting.lean).

## Results in the proof library

The harmonic classification at a unit idempotent identifies the harmonic eikonal cubics with eikonal constant one and dimension at least three with the real, complex, quaternionic and octonionic Hermitian models. The complex complete system of planes is unique up to one unitary conjugation and relabelling. The field defect theorem gives an attained positive deviation from constant unit-sphere energy for each excluded field count, a seven-field example with defect $`3/4`$, and a positive lower bound on the maximum cross-overlap discrepancy for six quaternionic frames. In the proof of the five-basis bound six unbiased frames would extend to seven. For a fixed pair, the other five shares would each exceed $`2/5`$, contradicting the pair sum. [Complex MUB classification, Brierley–Weigert–Bengtsson, §2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4097v2), [Field defect](src/ThirteenPlanes/FieldDefect/Main.lean), [Five-basis bound](src/ThirteenPlanes/Quaternionic/CapacityCorollaries.lean).

At 52 positive shares every share is zero or one. Every pair then lies in exactly one quartet, and the quartets are the projective lines of a projective plane isomorphic to $`\mathrm{PG}(2,3)`$. The proof library also establishes the converses from these incidence conditions. The printed proof uses the projective-plane classification from Hall’s *Combinatorial Theory*, second edition, and Hughes and Piper’s *Projective Planes*. The compact real form of Tits’s first construction of the Albert algebra (Tits’s second construction for $`M_3(\mathbb C)`$ with conjugate transposition) supplies Fourier idempotents, the multiplication table of the clock-and-shift (Weyl) matrices on each projective line and a cube-root phase formula for the cubic. Two orthogonal planes generate a complex Hermitian Jordan subalgebra through their frames. Adding a third orthogonal plane gives a complex Hermitian, quaternionic Hermitian or full Albert subalgebra. For each pair in a complete system, at least seven of the eleven choices of third plane generate the full Albert algebra. Rotating the four frames of a quartet of the graded system by an automorphism of their Hermitian subalgebra preserves completeness, and preserves the minimum precisely when it permutes the four planes of the quartet. [Incidence](src/ThirteenPlanes/Shares/ThirteenIncidence.lean), [Generation](src/ThirteenPlanes/AlbertGrading/Generation/NativePlanes.lean), [Quartet rotations](src/ThirteenPlanes/AlbertGrading/Recut/NativeRotations.lean).

The Cartan subalgebras of the compact complex and quaternionic matrix Lie algebras fix traceless diagonal planes up to unitary conjugacy. A Casimir identity turns an orthogonal decomposition into Cartan subalgebras, orthogonal for the Killing form, into an orthogonal sum of their fixed planes (zero weight planes). In particular an orthogonal decomposition of compact $`\mathfrak{sp}(3)`$ (type $`C_3`$, dimension 21) into Cartan subalgebras would yield seven unbiased quaternionic frames. For $`\mathfrak{sp}_6(\mathbb C)`$ the obstruction applies to such decompositions permuted by a compact conjugation. For compact $`\mathfrak f_4`$, every Cartan subalgebra is of frame type. It fixes the two-dimensional plane of a frame on the traceless Albert representation, with operator-trace Casimir one sixth of the projector onto its orthogonal complement. The thirteen zero weight planes of an orthogonal decomposition of compact $`\mathfrak f_4`$ into Cartan subalgebras are closed and fill the representation orthogonally. Their frames are pairwise unbiased. The grading of the complexified Albert algebra by $`\mathbb Z_3^3`$ gives one such decomposition, and rotated quartets yield inequivalent decompositions when they change the planes of the quartet. In the cyclic octonion coordinates $`(2,3),(3,1),(1,2)`$ the diagonal-frame stabilizer obeys $`(\alpha_1 a)(\alpha_2 b)=\overline{\alpha_3(\overline{ab})}`$. [Zero weight planes](src/ThirteenPlanes/Cartan/F4/Corollaries.lean), [Symplectic obstruction](src/ThirteenPlanes/Cartan/ClassicalSymplecticObstruction.lean), [Knapp, Theorem 3.6](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~aknapp/pdf-files/1-27.pdf), [Yokota, Theorem 2.6.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.0431), [Elduque, Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.2737).

For the compact second row, the theorem grants the identification given by Tits’s magic-square construction. It gives isomorphisms of real Lie algebras to $`\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)`$, $`\mathfrak{su}(6)`$ and the fixed real algebra of any compact conjugation on complex Chevalley $`E_6`$. The real Killing forms are negative definite, and the ranks are four, five and six. The extension is a bijection between orthogonal decompositions of the fixed algebra into Cartan subalgebras and those of the row algebra with each summand stable under the involution, orthogonality being for the Killing form. [Second-row theorem](src/ThirteenPlanes/Cartan/SecondRow/CompactE6.lean).

## Proof sketch

Polarizing the eikonal condition at a unit idempotent of the cubic product gives the Peirce decomposition of the cubic. A plane exposes three joint eigenspaces with a bilinear product composing norms. Hurwitz’s theorem restricts their dimensions and the cubic written at the unit idempotent identifies the Hermitian models. For a pair in a complete system of planes of an eikonal cubic, the product space lies in the orthogonal complement of the pair and has dimension four. The remaining plane projectors resolve the identity on that complement, so their half-traces against the projector onto the product space add to two. The quaternionic trace formula reduces the lower bound to an inequality for the balances of the three phases in the normal form. For thirteen planes, the total share is 52. The upper bound of one forces at least 52 positive terms and forces each positive term to be one at equality.

In the Albert orbit proof the four-dimensional product space identifies the other two planes in the quartet through a pair. In the compact real form of Tits’s first construction, an automorphism normalizes a pair of a reference quartet to the diagonal and Fourier frames. An automorphism fixing the subalgebra generated by the quartet puts a frame outside it in standard position. The eigenframes of the order-three elements on the projective lines through that frame determine the remaining planes. For completion, the two-dimensional orthogonal complement of the twelve traceless planes is closed. The proof diagonalizes one element and places both generators in the same frame. [Orbit recognition](src/ThirteenPlanes/AlbertGrading/Recognition/CoreMain.lean), [Completion](src/ThirteenPlanes/AlbertGrading/Completion/PairFrame.lean).

For the pencil, extrema recover the planes in the unlabelled case, polarized mixed responses recover the ratio, and product compatibility forces a common sign. The Lean witness at the endpoints reverses the cubic on one plane by an orthogonal pullback. In the Cartan construction the operator trace pairs opposite derivation grades and compact conjugation exchanges the grades in each pair. Their real descents give the thirteen Cartan subalgebras. Tensor contractions restrict the one-loop equation to the two pencil coefficients, leaving an equation for the ratio and one for the coupling constant.

## Mathematical sources

Cartan classified the harmonic eikonal cubics. Tkachev established the reducible normal form for the nonharmonic case. Hurwitz’s composition theorem limits the possible dimensions of the joint eigenspaces at a unit idempotent, and the Lean argument uses the unital composition-algebra form discussed by Baez. The formal proof of Hurwitz’s theorem comes from Ehrlich’s composition-algebra library through Albert Sequential Measurements. Tkachev spells the condition “eiconal”. [Cartan, Part I, §I](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01580289), [Tkachev, Theorem 1.1 and §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.2387v2), [Hurwitz, pp. 309–316](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN252457811_1898/LOG_0034.pdf), [Baez, §1.1, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0105155), [Ehrlich’s library, Palomar v1](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-08-29-000018&version=1), [Pinned adaptation notes](https://github.com/macgregor-sudo/albert-sequential-measurements/blob/10e5ca8ce341a1cb3a812569d3081005a40007c8/docs/ATTRIBUTION.md#composition-algebras).

Cohn, Kumar and Minton constructed the tight 39-point code as thirteen unbiased Albert frames. Griess constructed the associated grading of the Albert algebra by $`\mathbb Z_3^3`$, and its uniqueness up to automorphism is described by Elduque and Kochetov. Draper and Martín González give Tits’s first construction of the Albert algebra, and Boykin, Sitharam, Tiep and Wocjan relate unbiased bases to orthogonal decompositions of Lie algebras into Cartan subalgebras in the complex case. The pointwise diagonal frame stabilizer calculation in cyclic octonion coordinates uses Yokota’s triality theorem. [Cohn–Kumar–Minton, Theorem 8.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3188v4), [Griess, (4.3)–(4.6)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(90)90176-O), [Elduque–Kochetov, §4.5 and Theorem 4.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.1462), [Draper–Martín González, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703840), [Boykin–Sitharam–Tiep–Wocjan](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0506089), [Yokota, §2.1 and Theorem 2.7.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.0431).
